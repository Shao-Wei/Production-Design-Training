The best places are **Architectural Digest**, **BAFTA Guru**, **SDSA / Set Decorators Society of America**, and **Decorating Pages**. AD is especially good when you want a designer to walk through a finished set and explain visual choices; BAFTA Guru is better for craft-level masterclasses; SDSA gives you “inside the set” interviews from the set decoration and production-design side; Decorating Pages is one of the better long-form interview sources because it is hosted by Emmy-winning set decorator Kim Wannop and focuses specifically on film/TV design. ([Architectural Digest][1])

For **video set breakdowns**, start with AD’s **“Notes on a Set”** and similar AD set-design videos. Good examples in current search results include **“The Crown Sets Explained by the Show’s Set Designer”**, **“How ‘Wicked’ Built Oscar-Winning Sets…”**, and set tours like **“Inside the Set of Bridget Jones: Mad About the Boy.”** These are useful because they tend to be concrete: why a room was shaped a certain way, what was built vs found, and how design supports character and tone. ([youtube.com][2])

For **career guidance and craft explanations**, BAFTA Guru is one of the strongest free sources. Their production design playlist includes items like **“Discussion on Production Design | BAFTA Film Craft Sessions”** and mini-masterclasses such as **Maria Djurkovic: Production Design Mini Masterclass**. These are less “house tour” and more “how the department thinks,” which is what you want if you are training yourself seriously. ([youtube.com][3])

For **deeper, long-form conversations**, use **Decorating Pages**. It has both a YouTube channel and podcast, and it specifically features production designers, set decorators, and related craft people discussing real projects. This is one of the best places to hear how professionals talk through research, collaboration, references, and problem-solving, not just final visuals. ([youtube.com][4])

For **set-specific written interviews and behind-the-scenes case studies**, use **SDSA’s “Inside the Set”** and AD’s set-design articles. SDSA’s official site is dedicated to set decoration in motion pictures and television, and its “Inside the Set” archive regularly pairs production designers with set decorators on specific projects. AD’s set-design section also has many project-focused articles that can help you study how designers justify choices. ([Set Decorators][5])

For **beginner-friendly explanations**, StudioBinder has accessible overview videos on what production designers do and how production design functions in filmmaking. I would use StudioBinder for orientation, then move quickly to BAFTA/AD/Decorating Pages once you want real professional reasoning. ([youtube.com][6])

A good way to use these, in order, is:

1. **AD video first** to understand one set visually.
2. **BAFTA Guru** next to understand the broader job and workflow.
3. **Decorating Pages or SDSA** after that for long-form professional thinking. ([youtube.com][2])

If you want a starter list, I would begin with these 6:

* **The Crown Sets Explained by the Show’s Set Designer** ([youtube.com][2])
* **How ‘Wicked’ Built Oscar-Winning Sets…** ([youtube.com][7])
* **Maria Djurkovic: Production Design Mini Masterclass** ([YouTube][8])
* **Discussion on Production Design | BAFTA Film Craft Sessions** ([youtube.com][9])
* **Decorating Pages Podcast / YouTube channel** ([youtube.com][4])
* **SDSA Inside the Set archive** ([Set Decorators][5])

When you watch them, do not just passively watch. Pause and write:

* what story problem the set solves
* what is character-specific
* what is world-specific
* what is built, dressed, or implied
* what palette/material choices are doing emotionally

That turns videos into actual training.

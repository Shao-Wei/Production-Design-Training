# Little Women Breakfast Scene: House Interior Extraction

Source: `little-women-2019.pdf`, script pages 28-32.

Scene range used:
- The girls preparing/rehearsing before Marmee enters.
- `EXT./INT. CONCORD. MARCH HOUSE. CONTINUOUS. 1861.`
- The Christmas breakfast request in the kitchen.
- The later return to `INT. MARCH DINING ROOM. LATE AFTERNOON. 1861.`

## Explicit Interior Spaces

- March house interior.
- A modest March family home.
- Door / front entry moment: Marmee opens the door and enters the house.
- Kitchen: the girls follow Marmee into the kitchen while talking.
- Dining room: later in the sequence, the women return to the March dining room.
- Window: the girls run to the window to look after hearing Mr. Laurence sent the feast.
- Piano area: Beth goes to the piano before Marmee enters.
- Seating / chair: Amy says she will fall into a chair if she cannot faint properly, then falls into a chair.

- Breakfast.
- Tea.
- Cakes made by Hannah and Beth.
- Sewing.
- Amy's portrait.
- Beth's doll, Joanna.
- Jo's rewritten pages / play pages.
- Costumes for the girls' play.
- A handmade or decorated hat.
- Old shoes painted blue.
- Piano.

- Blankets, sweaters, and medicine brought to the Hummel house.

- Candy.
- Ice cream.
- More cakes.

## Activity Cues That Define The Interior

- The girls are laughing, rehearsing, and playing make-believe inside the March house.
- The room supports rough, physical play: Jo tackles Amy and Beth and pulls in Meg.
- Hannah enters and interrupts the play-fighting.
- The girls untangle and run to Hannah, suggesting open shared space rather than a formal, restricted room.
- The girls crowd around Marmee when she enters.
- Marmee's entrance turns the interior into a family gathering point.
- The girls move with Marmee into the kitchen, so the house interior is connected and active.
- The kitchen holds both domestic work and family conversation.
- Breakfast is visible enough for Meg to call attention to it.
- The breakfast becomes a shared moral object when Marmee asks them to give it away.
- Later, the dining room is arranged by Hannah before the women return.
- The feast is immediately visible on entry, making the dining room a reveal space.
- The window becomes a shared point of focus when the girls run to look outside.

## Implied Production Design Notes

- The March home should feel modest rather than grand.
- The interior should support overlapping uses: play rehearsal, sewing, eating, piano practice, family greeting, and charitable preparation.
- The room should feel communal and flexible, not like a single-purpose parlor.
- Props should look handmade, reused, or domestic rather than luxurious.
- The girls' creative life should be visible through papers, costumes, painted shoes, a doll, and the piano.
- The breakfast table / kitchen area should feel warm and desirable enough that giving it away has emotional weight.
- The dining room feast should contrast with the earlier modest breakfast: surprising, abundant, and almost magical to the girls.
- Hannah's presence should be readable through practical domestic order: food prepared, feast arranged, household movement managed.

## Design-Relevant Contrast In The Script

- March household: described through movement, noise, hunger, play, family closeness, and "merry mayhem."
- Laurence dining room: described as formal, quiet, proper, and the opposite of the March household.
- Hummel house: described as a near-collapsing shack, wretched, freezing, and impoverished.

This contrast helps define the March interior by comparison: it is modest, but alive, warm, socially loose, and home-like.
